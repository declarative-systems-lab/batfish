<br>1. ISP1 advertises two routes to its eBGP peer R1:
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;* 192.0.2.0/24
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;* 198.51.100.0/24
<br>2. R1 applies the inbound route policy R1_IN_FROM_ISP1 to control the routes received from ISP1 and their attributes, and sets the community value:
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;* 192.0.2.0/24 (with 400:100 community)
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;* 198.51.100.0/24 (with 500:200 community)
<br>3. Since the two network prefixes have only one available route, R1 selects both routes as the best routes.
<br>4. R1 applies the outbound route policy R1_OUT_TO_Customer to control the routes advertised to Customer and their attributes, allowing only routes with the 400:100 community:
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;* 192.0.2.0/24 (with 400:100 community)
<br>5. Therefore, Customer receives the ISP1-advertised prefix 192.0.2.0/24.
